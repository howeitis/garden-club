  Black-eyed Susan is a biennial or short-lived perennial: individual plants bloom hard for a season or two and then fade. Let a few flower heads go to seed and new rosettes fill in on their own, so a patch carries on even as its plants come and go.

  Leave the dark seed cones standing into winter and the goldfinches will find them. It's an easy first native for a sunny border or a new meadow planting, shrugging off heat, drought and poor soil.

A cheerful, drought-tolerant wildflower with bright golden-yellow petals surrounding a dark brown central cone. It thrives in full sun and well-drained Piedmont soils, self-sows readily, and provides long-lasting summer color while attracting pollinators.
