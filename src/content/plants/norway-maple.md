more: |-
  Norway maple was a favorite street tree for much of the 20th century because it tolerates pollution, compacted soil and hard conditions. Those same traits let it escape into woodlands, where its dense shade and shallow, fibrous roots leave little room for wildflowers, shrubs or young native trees beneath it. A lawn under a Norway maple rarely thrives for the same reason.

  When an old one comes down, a native red maple, sugar maple or blackgum makes a fine replacement — with far better fall color.

A non-native shade tree that outcompetes native trees, such as sugar maples, due to its dense shade and rapid growth.
