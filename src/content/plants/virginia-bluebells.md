  Virginia bluebells are spring ephemerals: they rise in early spring, bloom for a few weeks, set seed and vanish by early summer, leaving no trace until the next year. Pair them with ferns or later perennials that will fill the gap. The buds open pink and turn sky blue as the flowers mature.

  Given moist, rich soil like the stream banks of the Brandywine Valley, they slowly spread into generous drifts. Divide or move them just as the foliage begins to yellow, while you can still see where they are.

A beloved spring ephemeral wildflower producing loose clusters of pendulous, trumpet-shaped flowers — pink in bud, opening to clear sky blue. Thriving in moist, rich woodland soils along streams throughout the Brandywine Valley, it naturalizes beautifully.
