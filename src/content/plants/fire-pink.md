more: |-
  Fire pink is common farther south and west, but Delaware sits at the edge of its range and the state ranks it critically imperiled in the wild. Never dig it from a natural area; buy nursery-propagated plants from a native plant nursery or a plant sale.

  In the garden it wants excellent drainage — a rocky slope, a wall pocket or the edge of a woodland path — and dislikes wet winter soil. Plants are short-lived but will self-sow where they're happy. Sticky hairs on the stems trap small insects, which gives fire pink and its relatives another common name: catchfly.

A woodland wildflower with brilliant scarlet, star-shaped blooms on slender stems — one of the few truly red native flowers of spring. Its long, tubular flowers are made for ruby-throated hummingbirds.
