  Delaware has claimed the American holly as its state tree since 1939, when the state led the nation in shipping holly wreaths. Hollies are either male or female: only females bear berries, and they need a male tree nearby to pollinate them. Ask the nursery which you're buying — one male will serve several females.

  Give it room and time. American holly grows slowly and resents being moved once established, so choose its spot with care. Its dense evergreen branches shelter birds all year, and the berries hang on well into winter, when they become food at the hungriest time of year.

Delaware's state tree, the American Holly is a slow-growing evergreen with glossy, spine-tipped leaves and bright red berries that persist through winter. It provides essential cover and food for songbirds and small mammals during the coldest months and is a striking specimen tree for native landscapes.
