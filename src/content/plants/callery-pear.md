more: |-
  Callery pear was planted across American suburbs from the 1960s on, mostly as the 'Bradford' variety, which was thought to set no fruit. Different varieties pollinate one another, though, and the small fruits they now produce are eaten by birds, which scatter the seeds along roadsides, fields and woodland edges. The wild seedlings grow into thorny thickets that crowd out native plants.

  Older Bradford pears are also notorious for splitting apart in storms, so replacing one is often overdue anyway. Serviceberry gives the same white spring bloom, followed by berries the birds (and people) enjoy.

Including the "Bradford" pear, these trees were widely planted for their spring flowers but spread aggressively and form dense, thorny thickets.
