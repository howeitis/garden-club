height: "6–10 ft"
light: "Full sun to part shade (more sun, more berries)"
soil: "Moist to wet, acidic; handles poor drainage"
wildlife: "Winter berries for cedar waxwings, catbirds, and thrushes; spring flowers for native bees"
more: |-
  Like American holly, winterberry comes as male and female plants, and only the females bear berries. Plant a male within about 40 to 50 feet; one male can pollinate six to ten females. Nurseries sell matched male varieties for each female, so ask which to pair with yours.

  Cut branches last for weeks indoors and make a native stand-in for the oriental bittersweet so often used in fall and holiday arrangements.
---

A deciduous native holly that drops its leaves in fall to reveal stems crowded with bright red berries, which hang on well into winter. A natural fit for wet spots and rain gardens, and a favorite of cedar waxwings and other winter birds.
