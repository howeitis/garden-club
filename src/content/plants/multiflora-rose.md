---
name: Multiflora Rose
scientificName: Rosa multiflora
type: invasive
image: /multiflorarose.webp
order: 40
identify: "Arching, thorny canes that form thickets up to 10 ft across. Clusters of small, white, fragrant flowers in late spring become small red hips that last through winter. The surest sign is a feathery, fringed stipule where each leaf stalk meets the stem."
remove: "Dig out young plants with as much root as possible, wearing heavy gloves and long sleeves. Established thickets resprout from the root crown, so cut the canes back, then dig out or treat the crown, and watch for new seedlings for several years."
alternatives:
  - "Carolina rose"
  - "Swamp rose"
  - "Arrowwood viburnum"
more: |-
  Multiflora rose came from East Asia in the 1800s as a rootstock for garden roses, and in the mid-1900s it was promoted to farmers as a "living fence" and wildlife cover. Birds love the hips, and a single plant produces enormous numbers of seeds that stay alive in the soil for years. It now fills pastures, hedgerows and forest edges throughout the region.

  Our native roses, Carolina rose for dry sunny spots and swamp rose for wet ones, give the same pink-to-white summer flowers and winter hips without the takeover.
