name: English Ivy
scientificName: Hedera helix
type: invasive
image: /englishivy.webp
order: 10
identify: "Evergreen vine with dark, glossy, three- to five-lobed leaves that climbs by tiny rootlets along the stem. It flowers and sets black berries only after it climbs — up trees, walls and fences."
remove: "On trees, cut every vine at shoulder height and again at the ground, then pull the ivy back about two feet from the trunk. Leave the upper vines to wither in place rather than tearing them off the bark. Pull ground mats when the soil is moist and bag the pieces: any stem left on the ground can root."
alternatives:
  - "Pennsylvania sedge"
  - "Wild ginger"
  - "Golden ragwort"
  - "Christmas fern"
more: |-
  English ivy came over with colonial settlers as an ornamental groundcover. On the ground it forms mats that smother wildflowers and tree seedlings. Once it climbs, it shades out a tree's own leaves and adds heavy, wind-catching weight that brings trees down in storms.

  Climbing ivy is also the only ivy that flowers and fruits, and birds carry its berries into nearby woods. Cutting the vines on your trees is the single most useful step: it stops the seed supply and saves the tree.

Introduced to the U.S. in the 1700s as an ornamental groundcover. It spreads rapidly, outcompetes native vegetation, and kills trees by creating dense, heavy, evergreen mats that choke out light and cause wind-shear damage.
