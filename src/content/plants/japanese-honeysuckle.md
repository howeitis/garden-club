name: Japanese Honeysuckle
scientificName: Lonicera japonica
type: invasive
image: /honeysuckle.webp
order: 50
identify: "Twining vine whose leaves often stay green through winter. Paired, fragrant, tubular flowers open white and fade to yellow from late spring through summer, followed by glossy black berries."
remove: "Pull vines and dig out rooted runners when the soil is moist; any piece left touching the soil can root. On large patches, cutting back repeatedly through the growing season weakens it over time."
alternatives:
  - "Coral honeysuckle"
  - "Virginia creeper"
  - "Virgin's bower"
more: |-
  Japanese honeysuckle's sweet scent is part of summer in the mid-Atlantic, which is how it spread so easily: it was sold for gardens and planted along roadsides to hold soil. Keeping its leaves into winter gives it a head start on native plants each spring, and it climbs over shrubs and small trees until they collapse beneath it.

  If you love a flowering vine on a fence, plant our native coral honeysuckle instead. Its scarlet trumpets are a hummingbird favorite, and it stays where you put it.
