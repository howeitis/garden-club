name: Oriental Bittersweet
scientificName: Celastrus orbiculatus
type: invasive
image: /oriental-bittersweet.webp
order: 70
identify: "Twining woody vine with glossy, nearly round leaves. Small clusters of flowers and fruit grow all along the stem where the leaves attach; in fall the yellow husks split to show scarlet berries. Our native American bittersweet fruits only at the branch tips."
remove: "Pull seedlings and dig out the bright orange roots. On trees, cut the vines at the base and again at shoulder height; cut stems resprout, so check twice a year. Bag the berries rather than composting them."
alternatives:
  - "Winterberry holly"
  - "American bittersweet"
  - "Virginia creeper"
more: |-
  Oriental bittersweet wraps trees so tightly it strangles them, and its weight pulls canopies down in snow and wind. Birds eat the berries all winter and spread the seeds widely, so one vine at a forest edge can seed a whole woodland.

  A note for arrangers: the berried stems are prized for fall wreaths and centerpieces, but every discarded arrangement can start new vines. Cut branches of winterberry holly give the same bright color. If you plant American bittersweet, buy it from a native plant nursery — the two are easily confused, and they hybridize.
---

A highly invasive vine that kills trees by girdling them and adding excessive weight, leading to windthrow.
