height: "10–20 ft vine on a support"
light: "Full sun for the best bloom; tolerates part shade"

more: |-
  Coral honeysuckle climbs by twining, so give it a trellis, arbor, or fence and a little guidance at first. Unlike its Japanese cousin it doesn't root along the ground or smother its neighbors, and it has little fragrance — its scarlet trumpets are made for hummingbirds rather than noses.

  The heaviest bloom comes in spring, with scattered flowers into summer. In a mild winter it may keep some of its blue-green leaves.
---

Our native honeysuckle: a well-behaved climbing vine with clusters of coral-red, trumpet-shaped flowers that ruby-throated hummingbirds seek out from their first spring arrival. The right choice for a fence or trellis where Japanese honeysuckle once grew.
