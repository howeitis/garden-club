more: |-
  Butterfly weed is a milkweed, and like its relatives it feeds monarch caterpillars — but unlike most milkweeds its stems carry clear sap rather than milky latex. A deep taproot makes it one of the most drought-tolerant plants in the garden, and one of the hardest to move, so set young potted plants where you want them to stay.

  Be patient in spring. Butterfly weed waits until the soil is thoroughly warm and is often the last plant in the bed to appear. Mark its spot in fall so it isn't dug up by mistake.

A compact milkweed with vivid orange flower clusters that add a striking splash of warm color to sunny gardens. Essential for monarch butterfly conservation as both a nectar source and larval host plant, and remarkably drought-tolerant once established.
