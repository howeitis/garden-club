identify: "Dense, spiny shrub 2–6 ft tall with small, spoon-shaped leaves in clusters and a sharp spine beneath each cluster. Small, bright red berries dangle along the stems into winter. Scratch a stem: the inner wood is bright yellow."
remove: "Pull or dig small plants, roots and all, wearing heavy gloves. Barberry leafs out before most native shrubs, so it's easy to spot in early spring when the soil is soft. It resprouts from roots left behind, so check the spot again the following year."

more: |-
  Japanese barberry was planted for decades as a tough, deer-proof hedge — and the deer's refusal to eat it is exactly what lets it take over the forest floor. Researchers at the Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station found far more Lyme-carrying blacklegged ticks in dense barberry than in woods without it. The thickets hold humidity and shelter the white-footed mice the ticks feed on, and clearing the barberry brought tick numbers down for years afterward.

  Winterberry holly offers the same bright red winter berries without the thorns or the ticks.

A popular ornamental shrub that dominates forest understories, creating dense thickets that alter soil chemistry and reduce native plant diversity.
