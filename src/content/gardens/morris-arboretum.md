  Siblings John and Lydia Morris began their estate, Compton, in Chestnut Hill in 1887 and filled it with trees collected from around the world. The arboretum Lydia left to the University of Pennsylvania in 1932 keeps their Victorian spirit — a mix of garden styles, grottoes, and follies.

  The fernery is a rare survivor of the Victorian fern craze: a glasshouse built in 1899 just for ferns, with a grotto, pools, and bridges inside.

The official arboretum of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania and a 92-acre living museum operated by the University of Pennsylvania. Morris Arboretum features more than 13,000 labeled plants, the only freestanding Victorian fernery left in North America, the Out on a Limb canopy walk, a swan pond, and one of the finest collections of mature trees in the region.
