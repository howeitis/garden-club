  John Bartram, a self-taught Quaker farmer and botanist, founded this garden in 1728 and shipped American plants to gardeners across Europe. His son William traveled the South collecting plants and writing the book that still bears his name, *Travels*.

  In 1765 the Bartrams found a small grove of an unknown tree along Georgia's Altamaha River. They grew it here and named it *Franklinia* for their friend Benjamin Franklin. It has not been seen in the wild since the early 1800s, and every Franklinia growing today descends from the Bartrams' plants.

The oldest surviving botanic garden in North America, established in 1728 by John Bartram on the banks of the Schuylkill River. The 50-acre National Historic Landmark preserves Bartram's 18th-century house, historic plant collections, riverfront meadows, and the legacy of America's pioneering botanist who introduced more than 200 native plants to the world.
