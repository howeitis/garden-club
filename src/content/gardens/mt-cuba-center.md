  Mt. Cuba was the home of Pamela and Lammot du Pont Copeland, who bought the farmland in 1935. By the 1960s wildflowers were becoming popular but couldn't be bought, so gardeners dug them from the woods; Mrs. Copeland began propagating natives on the estate for local nurseries instead. Today Mt. Cuba is devoted to the plants of the Appalachian Piedmont — our own region — and to conserving them.

  For gardeners, the most useful part may be the research. The Trial Garden grows native species and their cultivars side by side for years and reports which ones perform best and which do the most for pollinators — practical guidance for choosing what to plant at home.

Dedicated to the study and conservation of native plants of the Appalachian Piedmont, Mt. Cuba Center features beautifully curated gardens and natural lands spanning over 1,000 acres. The gardens showcase the beauty of native wildflowers, ferns, and trees, and the center conducts important plant trials to help gardeners choose the best native cultivars.
